# Theory, Construct, Mechanism, Domain, Outcome — Do Not Conflate

## Theory
A general explanatory system specifying constructs, relationships, assumptions, and often boundary conditions.

## Construct
An abstract concept represented theoretically and often operationalized empirically.

## Mechanism
The process through which an antecedent/intervention changes an outcome.

## Domain / instrument / intervention
A category of policy, institution, technology, organizational practice, or contextual condition. It is not automatically a mechanism.

## Outcome
The explanandum: behavior, decision, performance, mobility, innovation, etc.

## Empirical regularity
A recurring association or pattern. It may motivate theory but is not itself a mechanism.

## Mechanism template

`Antecedent → intermediate state/opportunity/constraint → decision or behavioral process → outcome`

Examples of intermediate states include legal feasibility, expected returns, uncertainty, access to resources, identity stability, network opportunity, or perceived risk. These are mechanisms only when linked into an explanatory process, not merely listed.

## Theory mapping fields

For each theory capture:

- theory name and aliases;
- level of analysis;
- focal explanandum;
- core constructs;
- causal logic;
- assumptions;
- predictions;
- boundary conditions;
- empirical operationalizations;
- competing theories;
- whether the focal paper genuinely uses the theory or merely cites it.
