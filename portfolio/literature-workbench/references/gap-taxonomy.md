# Research Gap Taxonomy

## Stronger forms of gap

### Theoretical integration gap
Two or more explanatory traditions address the same phenomenon but generate incompatible or disconnected predictions.

### Mechanism gap
An association is established, but the process linking cause and outcome is untested, under-specified, or contested.

### Boundary-condition gap
Effects vary by context, actor, time, institutional regime, field, career stage, etc., but theory does not explain when/why.

### Construct / measurement gap
Key concepts are conflated, proxied poorly, or measured inconsistently across studies.

### Identification / method gap
Existing designs cannot support the causal or dynamic claim they routinely make.

### Temporal / dynamic gap
The literature treats a process as static despite sequencing, duration, adaptation, hysteresis, or path dependence.

### Contradiction / anomaly gap
High-quality studies produce divergent results not resolved by existing theory.

### Scope / context gap
A theoretically meaningful population or institutional regime is absent, and there is a reason to expect different mechanisms—not merely because it has not been studied.

## Weak gap statements to avoid

- “Few studies have examined X.”
- “No studies combine A and B.”
- “Most studies focus on Western countries.”

These become meaningful only after explaining why the omission changes theoretical expectations or empirical identification.
