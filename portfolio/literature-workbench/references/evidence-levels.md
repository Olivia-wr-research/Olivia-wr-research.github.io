# Evidence Levels E0–E4

The level describes how directly a recorded statement is anchored in accessible source evidence. It is not a generic study-quality score.

| Level | Meaning | Permitted use |
|---|---|---|
| **E0 Direct** | Directly verifiable source fact/result/statement with a precise locator (page, section, table, figure, appendix, dataset field, or equivalent). | May be stated as source evidence with appropriate citation. |
| **E1 Explicit** | The authors explicitly make the conceptual/theoretical/mechanism claim, but it is not directly tested or the exact empirical support is indirect. A locator is still required. | Attribute to the authors; do not upgrade to “demonstrates/proves.” |
| **E2 Synthesized** | A cross-source or within-paper synthesis that is well supported by multiple E0/E1 elements but is not itself a verbatim/explicit author claim. | Present as synthesis: “Taken together…”, “The evidence suggests…”. |
| **E3 Interpreted** | Analyst inference that is plausible and evidence-consistent but not explicitly stated/tested by the source(s). | Label as interpretation; do not present as established literature finding. |
| **E4 Proposed** | New conjecture, hypothesis, conceptual extension, or speculative link. | Use only as proposed framework/research agenda; never as prior evidence. |

## Mandatory rules

1. E0/E1 require a source locator whenever the source format permits one.
2. A DOI alone is not a locator for a claim.
3. E2 must name the supporting paper IDs or claims.
4. E3/E4 cannot populate `authors_claim`.
5. If full text was not accessed, do not assign E0 to page-level claims from abstracts/metadata.
6. A high-quality study can still contain an E1 theoretical assertion; evidence level and study quality are separate dimensions.
