# Claim–Citation Audit Rules

Audit every material claim in a final draft.

## Checks

### Support
Does at least one cited source support the exact proposition, not merely the topic?

### Scope
Is the claim broader than the population, country, period, method, or outcome in the cited evidence?

### Causality
Does the wording imply causal identification when the source is descriptive/associational?

### Attribution
Is an analyst synthesis accidentally written as an author claim?

### Locator
For strong or specific claims, is there a page/section/table/figure/appendix locator where feasible?

### Consensus language
Words such as “consistently,” “widely,” “established,” “robustly,” and “the literature shows” require multi-source support and contradiction checks.

### Contradiction
Are credible null/negative/conflicting findings omitted?

### Citation bundling
Do all sources at the end of a sentence actually support the full sentence? Split the claim if not.

### Secondary citation
If source B cites source A for a foundational proposition, prefer verifying A before treating B as evidence for A's exact claim.

## Severity

- CRITICAL: fabricated/mismatched citation, nonexistent access, material false claim.
- MAJOR: overgeneralization, causal overclaiming, omitted contradiction that changes interpretation.
- MODERATE: weak locator, ambiguous attribution, construct drift.
- MINOR: formatting/consistency issue with no substantive effect.
