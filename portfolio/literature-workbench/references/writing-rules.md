# Literature Review Writing Rules

## Paragraph architecture

Prefer:

1. synthesis claim;
2. evidence across multiple studies;
3. variation/contradiction/boundary condition;
4. theoretical implication or transition.

Avoid one-paper-one-sentence catalogs unless providing historical chronology.

## Organizing logics

Choose the one that best matches the question:

- theory-driven;
- mechanism-driven;
- debate/contradiction-driven;
- process/stage-driven;
- multilevel;
- historical development;
- methods/measurement-driven.

## Language calibration

- “is associated with” for associational evidence;
- “predicts” for model prediction, not necessarily causal effect;
- “increases/decreases” only when design and source support causal or temporal interpretation;
- “suggests/is consistent with” for E2–E3 synthesis;
- “we propose” for E4 framework components.

## Do not use filler novelty

Replace “there is limited research” with a specific unresolved theoretical, mechanism, measurement, identification, dynamic, contradiction, or boundary-condition problem.
