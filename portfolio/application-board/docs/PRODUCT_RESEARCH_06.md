# Product research: discovery, bilingual experience and recurring value

Reviewed 2026-10-15. This is desk research using official product pages. No user interviews were conducted. User scenarios and business recommendations below are hypotheses, not validated demand or willingness to pay.

## Evidence from comparable products

| Product | Observed capabilities | Implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [FindAPhD](https://www.findaphd.com/about/about-us) | Opportunity search and comparison, funding and application advice | Show useful opportunities immediately; attach guidance to decisions. |
| [FindAPhD account and newsletter](https://www.findaphd.com/signups/) | Free weekly subject updates, saved projects and enquiry tracking | Basic alerts and saved opportunities already have free alternatives. |
| [Academic Positions](https://academicpositions.com/) | Search by field, position, country and employer; alerts; separate interface-language and job-language settings | English interfaces must not remove non-English advertisements; Chinese explanations should retain original titles and links. |
| [AcademicTransfer](https://www.academictransfer.com/en/) | CV Match, Research Match, career exploration and supervisor discovery | CV matching alone is not a unique value proposition. |
| [EURAXESS](https://euraxess.ec.europa.eu/) | Free accounts, saved jobs/searches, new and expiring opportunity alerts | Sending users to a general official search portal does not complete their information-gathering task. |

AcademicTransfer's [Career Navigator](https://www.academictransfer.com/en/career-navigator/) explains tasks, skills, possible next steps and personal experiences across research careers. Its CV and research matching links were inspected, but matching quality was not tested with a submitted CV. Public counts change over time and are not evidence of exhaustive coverage or comparable doctoral-vacancy totals.

## User scenarios to validate

1. A time-constrained applicant wants relevant, current opportunities gathered in one place. Measure time to a useful shortlist and interference from duplicates or expired offers.
2. An applicant can describe previous work but is uncertain about future interests. Start from experience and transferable methods; let the person explore adjacent fields and correct suggestions. Measure whether they discover a relevant possibility and understand why it was shown.
3. An active applicant needs to compare requirements, materials and dates across a shortlist. Preserve progress and show a clear next action. This is a later stage of the first two scenarios, not a separate product.

These scenarios guide interaction design; they do not restrict the database to particular disciplines, nationalities or personal backgrounds.

## Product priorities

**P0: dependable supply.** Establish source ingestion, normalized records, deduplication, status checks and employer links. Count imported, usable opportunities separately from source portals and institutions. Adding a portal link does not add its jobs to this product.

**P0: bilingual application experience.** Make opportunities visible on arrival. Use a small navigation structure such as Discover, Saved and Applications. Keep technical collection terminology in secondary documentation. Cards should explain the research, location, funding type and deadline. Keep original-source access close to the decision.

**P1: inspectable matching.** Explain relevant experience, requirements needing confirmation and adjacent possibilities. Permit editing and reversal. Avoid interpreting keyword overlap as admission probability or eligibility. When results are empty, make widening the search easy and show what changes.

**P1: coherent interactions.** Use consistent details, immediate saved-state feedback, persistent filters and restored scroll positions. A compact desktop and mobile experience matters more than adding dashboards or warnings.

**P2: task-based user observation.** Once supply and the main workflow work, observe people finding and comparing opportunities. Ask them to carry out realistic tasks rather than to perform product analysis. Invitations and traffic do not substitute for useful results.

## Commercial evidence and hypotheses

[Academic Positions job advertising](https://recruit.academicpositions.com/job-advertising/) lists Essential at EUR 690, Reach at EUR 1,490 and Impact at EUR 2,490. Its [institutional subscriptions](https://recruit.academicpositions.com/subscriptions/) include recruitment integrations and reporting; a uniform subscription price was not shown. [FindAPhD advertising](https://www.findaphd.com/providers/) includes listings, display advertising, email campaigns and events, with prices stated as starting at GBP 110. These are observed competitor offers, not estimates of what this product can charge.

Keep search, basic filters, bilingual explanations, official application links, saving and basic matching free. Candidate subscriptions could be tested for ongoing cross-source monitoring, meaningful change detection and application planning, but only after demonstrating repeated time savings. Basic alerts alone face strong free competition.

Institutional subscriptions could eventually fund job synchronization, recruitment pages, clearly labelled promotion and aggregate performance reporting. They require evidence of relevant candidate reach. Paid exposure should not silently change natural matching results. Do not introduce a payment system before the service delivers recurring value.

Lifetime access is a poor default for a service with ongoing data-maintenance costs. A candidate plan should be easy to pause or cancel because job search is episodic. Pricing and conversion remain unvalidated.

## Growth sequence

First make specific opportunity pages useful and shareable. Then create genuinely populated country and subject views and updates containing real new opportunities. Assess useful shortlists, return use and progression to official applications before buying traffic. Any analytics should avoid collecting CV contents or sensitive application materials.

The proposed promise is: bring scattered doctoral opportunities together, explain them clearly, and help people choose their next step. A feeling of discovery comes from finding a real possibility with a comprehensible reason and an achievable next action.
